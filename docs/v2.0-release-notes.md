# v2.0.0 — Solana

Aegis runs on two chains. The vault and the oracle network exist on Solana as programs of their own,
indexed into the same database and served by the same API. Governance stays on Arbitrum and reaches
Solana through Wormhole: a proposal passed there is dispatched, received by a Solana program, held for
a delay, and executed with measured limits. The UI serves both chains.

As before: read the second section before the first.

---

## What v2.0 adds

**The Solana vault.** An Anchor program with the Arbitrum vault's share accounting:
- virtual shares and assets, floored in both directions;
- a deposit cap and a minimum deposit;
- pause and a withdrawal freeze;
- slippage floors on deposit and withdrawal;
- a two-step admin handover.

It holds idle assets only; there is no strategy on either chain to port.

**The Solana oracle.** Nodes stake an SPL token and submit Ed25519-signed values, verified on chain
through the Ed25519 program. Rounds settle to the same median as on Arbitrum, and outliers and missed
rounds are judged and slashed by the same Go services, now running per chain. Node signing, submission
verification, and slashing all go through `internal/chain/svm`, alongside the EVM implementation behind
the same interfaces.

**Governance across chains.**
- **On Arbitrum.** A proposal whose action targets another chain is refused at proposal time if no
  route exists. Once queued and past its delay, the Timelock dispatches it through
  `WormholeDispatcher` and Wormhole's core, and the operation reads `DISPATCHED`. Nothing runs locally.
- **On Solana.** The governance receiver verifies the guardians' signatures through Wormhole's
  verify-VAA shim. It then checks the emitter, message version and target cluster, and holds the
  message for a delay of at least an hour.
- **Limits on execution.** When the delay has passed, anyone may execute the message as the receiver's
  authority. The call is limited by:
  - an allowlist of target programs;
  - a hash of the account list the DAO voted on;
  - caps on what actually leaves the registered treasury, per message and over an exact rolling window.
- **Guardian and governance.** A guardian key can pause the receiver and cancel a waiting message. Every
  parameter, and lifting a pause, belongs to governance.

**Indexing and the API across chains.**
- **Chain registry.** Every chain has one registered id; services refuse any other.
- **Transaction identifiers.** Each is stored in its chain's canonical form: EVM hashes, 64-byte Solana
  signatures.
- **Choosing a chain.** One API serves several chains, and a request names its chain with `?chain=`.
- **Solana events.** They are read from finalized transactions and decoded from each program's committed
  IDL, and failed transactions are skipped.
- **Remote governance.** The receiver's messages are indexed into `governance_remote_actions`. A
  dispatched proposal read from Arbitrum carries its outcome on Solana, and Solana serves
  `/v1/governance/remote-actions`.

**The UI on both chains.**
- **New design system.** Every page is rebuilt on it: shadcn/ui, Material UI data grids, and one token
  palette in dark and light.
- **Chain switcher.** It keeps the chain in the URL and offers only the modules each chain has.
- **Solana vault.** Deposits and withdrawals are signed by Solana wallets. A test wallet is offered on
  `solana-localnet` only.
- **Governance timeline.** A dispatched proposal shows each step through Wormhole to execution or
  cancellation on Solana, with a live countdown to its delay.
- **Received governance.** Solana lists the actions it received, each linked back to its proposal.

## What v2.0 does not change

Everything the earlier release notes say the protocol is not, it still is not:

- **No external audit.** The v1.0 internal review is the authors reviewing their own work. The Solana
  programs and the dispatcher were reviewed the same way, by their authors.
- **No deployment, anywhere.** Nothing is deployed to any Solana cluster or any Arbitrum network.
- **No mainnet, no funds, no custody.** Keys in this repository and its tests are test keys.
- **No relayer service.** A dispatched message reaches Solana only when someone relays it; no service
  does. Recorded in `DEFERRED.md`.
- **The zk gate is on Arbitrum only.** Its verifier generator emits Solidity, and verifiers here are
  generated, never hand-written.
- **No proposal creation, oracle staking, or node registration in the UI**, as before.

## Breaking changes

**The Timelock no longer refuses remote operations.**
- **Before:** one reverted with `CrossChainDispatchUnavailable`.
- **Now:** it is dispatched, and the operation takes a new state, `DISPATCHED`, appended to the enum.
- **New surface:** `OperationDispatched` and `DispatcherUpdated` events, a `dispatcher()` getter, and
  `setDispatcher`.
- **Governor:** it refuses a remote action with `NoRouteForChain` at proposal time, and a `DISPATCHED`
  proposal cannot be cancelled.
- **Storage:** the dispatcher slot is carved from the Timelock's `__gap`, which shrinks from 40 to 39.
  Every other slot keeps its place, and the layout check confirms the v2.0.0 baseline is a valid
  successor of v0.4.0.
- **Upgrading:** a deployed system needs UUPS upgrades of the Timelock and Governor, a new
  `WormholeDispatcher`, and `setDispatcher` and `setRoute` by the admin (`docs/role-migration.md`).

**The oracle node cap is enforced everywhere (ORC-2).**
- **The defect:** `stake()` and `cancelUnstake()` reactivated nodes without checking `maxNodes`, and the
  submission limit was declared but never enforced.
- **Now:** every reactivation checks for room, `maxNodes` above 31 reverts with `MaxNodesAboveCeiling`,
  and a submission beyond the limit reverts with `SubmissionLimitReached`.
- **Existing deployments:** one configured above 31 must lower the cap before upgrading.

**Every service refuses an unregistered chain id.** `CHAIN_ID` must be one of the six ids in
`backend/pkg/types/chain.go`. Before, any positive number was accepted.

**An API serving several chains needs the chain named.** With more than one chain in `API_CHAINS`, a
request without `?chain=` is refused rather than answered for a chain chosen for it. A single-chain API
behaves as before.

**One migration.** `000011` adds `governance_remote_actions`. It creates only; nothing existing changes.

**Stack deviations, chosen for this release and recorded in `docs/v2.0-solana-plan.md` §21:**
- Material UI and 21st.dev components beside the spec's Tailwind-only frontend;
- the Solana wallet adapter and `@solana/web3.js` beside Wagmi, Viem and RainbowKit;
- a `localnet` build of the receiver whose delay floor is 5 seconds, loaded only by the end-to-end
  validator, whose clock cannot be advanced.

## Defects found while building v2.0

Some surfaced by running against real chains or a browser, and some by reading code before building on
it. None had been caught by the tests that existed.

| Defect | How it surfaced |
|---|---|
| The Arbitrum node cap could be exceeded by cycling unstake, register and cancel, leaving the settlement sort unbounded (ORC-2). | Porting the rule to Solana, where it was designed in |
| A `DISPATCHED` proposal could be cancelled, recording as stopped a message that had already left. | Writing the dispatcher's state transitions |
| As designed, a pause on the receiver could never be lifted: unpausing was governance's alone, and a pause blocked governance's own messages. | Implementing §18.4 |
| Solana's chain ids are above 2^53, so all three clusters parse to one JavaScript number. | Building the chain registry in the UI |
| The indexer's staking handler failed on round events from the same Solana program, and an unstake cancellation marked a node active. | End-to-end tests of the Solana oracle |
| The local validator pruned the history a Solana test indexes from, and the indexer rightly refused the range. | The full end-to-end suite, once this release's larger transactions were in it |
| The backend's `Proposal` gained `remote` one commit before the frontend mirrored it, and Frontend CI's drift check failed that commit. | CI, fixed by the next commit |
| A remembered Solana wallet, the stored theme, and RainbowKit's theme each broke hydration. | The browser |

## Checked against real chains

- **Governance across chains.** A proposal voted on Anvil was dispatched through Wormhole's real EVM
  core, signed by the test guardian, relayed through Wormhole's mainnet shim and core bridge on
  `solana-test-validator`, and executed after its delay. The Solana vault's admin became the receiver's
  authority. Both indexers and the API reported it executed.
- **Refusals on the way.** A message signed outside the guardian set, one from another emitter, and an
  execution before the delay were each refused.
- **Mutations against the whole stack.** Removing the emitter check, indexing an execution as a
  cancellation, not attaching the outcome to the proposal, and voting a different account list each
  failed that test.
- **A Solana deposit.** One made through the Solana program was indexed and served beside an Arbitrum
  deposit. A failed transaction's deposit event was not indexed.
- **A Solana oracle round.** It was submitted by the Go node, settled on chain, aggregated, and slashed
  by the Go services.
- **In a browser.** The UI's test wallet deposited into and withdrew fully from a Solana vault on the
  local validator. The vault's token account held exactly what the UI reported.

## Verification

367 contract tests, 93 Solana tests, 21 circuit tests, 26 zk service tests, 142 frontend tests, 45
end-to-end tests, 16 backend packages. Slither clean.

**Storage layouts.** They are recorded as v2.0.0 baselines, including `WormholeDispatcher`'s first.
Against v0.4.0, only the Timelock changed. The Solana programs' account layouts are baselined in
`solana/layouts`, and may change only by carving fields from `reserved`.

**Drift checks added in this release:**
- the Solana IDLs against a fresh build, and the backend's and frontend's embedded copies against them;
- the Solana account layouts;
- the EVM layout rule, now a tested script;
- Wormhole's committed Solana binaries against their pinned hashes;
- the frontend's chain registry against `chain.go`;
- the design tokens against `globals.css`.

Each was confirmed to fail when the thing it guards was broken. Mutation checks ran against the Solana
programs, the dispatcher, the receiver, the indexer and the UI, and every surviving mutation was either
covered by a new test or shown equivalent.

## Accepted risks

Seventeen entries in `DEFERRED.md`, seven of them new in v2.0:
- the Solana vault has no strategy;
- the zk gate is on Arbitrum only;
- Solana oracle accounts are never closed;
- governance messages are relayed by hand;
- tokens outside a registered treasury are not capped;
- received messages are never closed;
- the receiver is built for Wormhole's mainnet program addresses.

ORC-2 moved to the decided table.
